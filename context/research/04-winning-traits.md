> **Second pass added 2026-10-03.** An independent second researcher (Codex, gpt-5.6-terra) added findings and corrections in the "Addendum" section at the end of this file. Where the addendum and the body disagree, the addendum is later; check its source-check status.

Research date: 2026-10-03. Research only. No product ideas in this file.

How to read the tables:

- "Demo moment" is the thing the project could show working, taken from the project's own writeup, the organiser's post, or press. Live-judging details are rarely documented. Where the source does not describe the live demo, the cell says so or is marked [U].
- "Continued as product?" is "[U]" unless a source says something concrete.
- [U] = I could not verify this against a source.
- Devpost winner lists come from each event's project gallery (winner-flagged projects). The gallery does not always show which prize each project won; the prize column comes from the project's own Devpost page where I fetched it.

## 4. Google Gemma challenges (Kaggle)

All three are online, video-judged competitions, not weekend events. Gemma 3n Impact Challenge criteria: Impact and Vision 40, Video Pitch and Storytelling 30, Technical Depth and Execution 30; the technical criterion asks whether the technology is real and "not just faked for the demo" (Kaggle page text via search). Gemma 4 Good uses the same three headings and weights: Impact and Vision 40, Video Pitch and Storytelling 30, Technical Depth and Execution 30, with the same "not just faked for the demo" wording (https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/gemma-4-good-hackathon/overview, read directly on 2026-10-03). A separate small "Gemma 4 Challenge" ran on DEV with a $3,000 pool (https://dev.to/challenges/google-gemma-2026-05-06); I did not review its winners.

## Traits winners share

Each trait lists at least two winners from the tables above.

1. **A tightly defined user with an acute, physical-world problem (common, not universal).** Shepherd (blind cane users; 1.7M legally blind Americans), Gemma Vision (the developer's blind brother), 3VA (Eva, who has cerebral palsy), Lucid Voice (people who lost their voice), pFOG (Parkinson's freezing of gait). Only Gemma Vision and 3VA name an actual individual; the others cite a population. Accessibility and health are heavily represented among TreeHacks 2025 and 2026 winners, and dominate Gemma 3n, MedGemma and Solve for Tomorrow 2025. Reviewer caveat: several verified top prizes went to projects without a disability or health user: FaceTimeOS (Cal Hacks 12.0 1st, general Mac users), HawkWatch (TreeHacks 2025 Grand Prize, security monitoring), GameSense (Qualcomm Bengaluru Grand Prize, game commentary), diffuji (TreeHacks 2026 Most Creative, novelty), OrgLens and CrossBeam (workplace and permitting tools).

2. **The product acts on the world, not only on a screen.** Shepherd's motor nudges the user; HeartStart compresses a chest; BrailleBot punches paper; Ted.AI responds to a hug; Peel dissolves a pill under LEDs; diffuji prints a sticker; GEM-4 drives a physical robot. Of the three overall grand prizes I could verify for the 2025-26 collegiate season, two used custom hardware (Shepherd at TreeHacks 2026, Peel at HackMIT 2026); the third, FaceTimeOS at Cal Hacks 12.0, did not, but the 2nd-place project there (Ted.AI) did. Reviewer caveat: this is a pattern of in-person collegiate events. The verified top winners of the online and edge contests were software on stock devices (Gemma Vision, Vite Vere Offline, EpiCast, GameSense, EdgeFit Coach, all five Korea winners), as was HawkWatch (TreeHacks 2025 Grand Prize).

3. **A closed sensor-to-action loop with a stated latency or rate.** Shepherd (60 fps depth, under 50 ms, 10 Hz BLE), EdgeFit Coach (13+ fps, under 100 ms), Mira (voice to 3D location in under 10 s; a cloud round trip, not a control loop). Theracat closes a motion-to-haptics loop without user action but states no latency or rate, and its live detector is a placeholder heuristic on a laptop, so it is weak evidence here. Shepherd's own numbers differ between Devpost (under 50 ms) and its README (under 100 ms; reaction under 150 ms).

4. **One headline number that frames the value.** Shepherd "about 1/20 the cost"; BrailleBot "under $15" against "$1,500"; Gem-Care WER 19.0% vs 32.7%; PreVillage 7.5 tokens/s on a Pi 5; pFOG 90%+ detection; GridVeda 98.09%. Reviewer caveat: all are self-reported. Only Gem-Care (baseline given), GridVeda (5-fold cross-validation, ± 0.80%) and Gilbeot (CER 13.14% to 5.00% on a 134-utterance held-out set) state how the number was measured. Shepherd's cost ratio and pFOG's ">90% accuracy" come with no stated method, and both still won.

5. **A one-sentence hook built on a new interaction, not a new model.** FaceTimeOS ("FaceTime your Mac"), diffuji ("a diffusion-powered instant camera"), EyeCraft (wink to jump in Minecraft), Robosurge ("Cursor for surgery"; winner-flagged in the TreeHacks 2026 gallery, prize not checked), Gilbeot (landmarks instead of metres). The Cal Hacks 12.0 winner names novelty of the idea as the first of his three reasons for winning (https://blog.dylanlu.com/cal-hacks-12/).

6. **AI is one narrow step inside a system that is mostly ordinary engineering (holds for the collegiate hardware winners; not for the Gemma winners, where the model is the product).** Shepherd's control loop is a gap-seeking algorithm plus colour heuristics with no LLM in it; Theracat's live detector is a simple motion-variability score (the Random Forest in its writeup lives in a notebook and is not yet wired in, per its README), with the LLM only in the voice layer; pFOG is a CNN-LSTM on IMU data; Project Rosie uses deterministic templates for the safety-critical output; Peel's core measurement is an optical sensor.

7. **Input in the user's natural modality: camera, voice, touch, motion. A typed chat box is rarely the only interface.** Gemma Vision (camera + controller + voice), Trido (voice whiteboard), Mira (voice on glasses), KawanIsyarat (signing to camera), Ted.AI (hug and pulse), Lucid Voice (two or three taps). Reviewer caveat: chat does appear in verified winners, so "not a chat box" is too strong. emerGen has a chat Q&A mode, E.M.Pilot has conversational email commands, EdgeFit Coach has an interactive chat, OmNom takes natural-language orders through a website, and CivicFix, CurePharma AI, Aarogya Assist and PreVillage are WhatsApp conversations. In most of these the chat sits beside voice or photo input, or lives in a channel the user already uses.

8. **Offline or local-first is part of the user story.** Vite Vere Offline, LENTERA, FieldScreen AI, BridgeDx, CodeBuddy, PenguinAgent, HorizonX, emerGen. In each, the user is somewhere connectivity or privacy rules out the cloud. Reviewer caveat: all but HorizonX come from contests that require or reward local inference (Gemma, MedGemma, Qualcomm), so this is partly a selection effect. At the collegiate events most verified top winners used cloud models (Mira, Peel, FaceTimeOS, HawkWatch, diffuji, Bob, Edith); the local-first exceptions there are HorizonX, GridVeda and Lucid Voice.

9. **Model adaptation is common among Gemma-family winners; it helps but is not required.** 3VA (MLX fine-tune for one user), Dream Assistant (Unsloth fine-tune on one voice), Gem-Care (fine-tune with WER reported), EpiCast and FieldScreen AI (fine-tuned MedGemma), emerGen (fine-tuned Llama 3.2 3B), PathOS (custom training with GRPO). Reviewer caveat: a measured result is in the sources only for Gem-Care (WER) and Gilbeot (CER); the others report the fine-tune without a metric. Several top winners did no fine-tuning: the Qualcomm blog says GameSense "did not fine-tune the model" and EdgeFit Coach's LLM "required no fine-tuning"; Gemma Vision (Gemma 3n 1st) and Vite Vere Offline (2nd) are described as deployments and ports, not fine-tunes.

10. **The writeup names the hardest bug and the pivot that fixed it.** Shepherd (corridor oscillation fixed by switching from repulsion to gap-seeking), KawanIsyarat (load and unload Whisper to fit memory; disable thinking mode), Lucid Voice (60 s delay cut by removing reasoning tokens), BrailleBot (foam, cloth, paper, then cork), HorizonX (three failed runtimes before a working one), GameSense (streaming from the GENIE C++ app). Reviewer caveat: Devpost's template has a "Challenges we ran into" section that every submission fills in, so this describes good writeups rather than separating winners from losers.

11. **Built with a real user (thin evidence; two solid examples, both from one online contest).** Gemma Vision (brother's feedback shaped features), 3VA (built for Eva). Both are Gemma 3n entries built over weeks, not during a weekend event. Project Lend claims 50+ lb of food delivered to shelters during the weekend (from its gallery tagline only; prize not checked). Lucid Voice only lists AAC-community testing as future work, and Shepherd's writeup describes no testing with blind users, so neither supports this trait. No verified weekend-hackathon winner documents testing with its target user during the event.

12. **Prize stacking through real use of sponsor or platform technology.** Shepherd (Grand + two OpenEvidence prizes), Peel (Grand + Elastic), Mira (Most Impactful + OpenAI track), Ted.AI (2nd overall + LiveKit), Gemma Vision (1st + Google AI Edge prize), Theracat (track grand prize + SkyDeck). The prizes are confirmed; whether the sponsor technology was load-bearing in each is [U] (clear for Gemma Vision and Peel's Elasticsearch pipeline; not checked for Shepherd's OpenEvidence use or Ted.AI's LiveKit use). Counter-signal: the FaceTimeOS author writes that the Fetch.ai agent in his 1st-place project was "kinda useless", so sponsor tech can be nominal in a winner.

13. **Demo and story are treated as engineering work.** Gemma challenges give 70 of 100 points to impact and video storytelling. The TreeHacks 2026 education-track winner says execution and presentation mattered as much as the implementation (https://www.evanyu.dev/blog/treehacks-2026). FaceTimeOS's author lists a polished demo with a user story as a winning factor. Judges at a June 2026 JetBrains event say winners lead with the problem and show the solution inside about 90 seconds (https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/).

14. **Local language and low-resource context, especially at India and global-south events.** CurePharma AI (regional-language prescriptions), MindBridge (regional Hindi dialects), PreVillage (Romanised Nepali), EpiCast (West African languages), CodeBuddy (Indonesia, paper notebooks).

15. **A public repo that someone else can run.** Qualcomm requires from-scratch setup instructions and a packaged executable; all five Korea winners and both Bengaluru winners have documented stacks; Shepherd, FaceTimeOS, Gemma Vision, Theracat, pFOG and Lucid Voice link public repos (the reviewer did not run any of them; Theracat's README says its live inference is still a placeholder).

## Traits specific to on-device / edge winners

Reviewer note (2026-10-03): "edge" in this section mostly does not mean "phone". Sorting the verified winners by where inference actually ran:

- Inference on a phone: HorizonX (Moondream on a Galaxy S24), Gemma Vision (Gemma 3n via MediaPipe), Gilbeot (Gemma 4 E2B via LiteRT-LM, tested on Galaxy S23 and S10e), KawanIsyarat (Gemma 4 E2B via Cactus on mid-range Android), MindBridge (mobile build with on-device benchmarks), Shepherd (ARKit depth + Apple Vision on an iPhone; classical CV, no LLM). Vite Vere Offline is offline with Gemma 3n; the device is not named in the blog.
- Phone used for interaction or capture only, inference elsewhere: Mira (Android phone relays glasses to cloud GPUs), FaceTimeOS (phone call to a Mac using cloud models), Peel (phone photo, cloud GPT-4o), Dragverse (phone scan; compute location [U]), CivicFix, CurePharma AI, Aarogya Assist and PreVillage (WhatsApp front end; PreVillage's model runs on a Raspberry Pi 5). EpiCast and Sunny are "mobile" apps whose inference location is [U].
- Edge but not a phone: every Qualcomm winner in section 5 (Snapdragon X Copilot+ PCs), Lucid Voice (Apple Silicon laptop), Trido (budget laptops), PenguinAgent, GridVeda (Jetson), LENTERA, Acuífero·Vigía (Pi 5). Theracat and pFOG run their classifiers on a host computer, not on the wearable.

So the NPU, partitioning and 3B-7B model evidence below comes from laptops; only the first bullet is direct evidence about phone inference.

1. **The edge constraint is justified by the use case.** GameSense: latency and per-player cloud cost. EdgeFit Coach and E.M.Pilot: privacy of camera frames and email. FieldScreen AI, BridgeDx, Gilbeot: no connectivity where the user is. The pitch explains why this cannot be a cloud app.

2. **Explicit compute partitioning with the NPU named.** GameSense assigns game logic to CPU, rendering to GPU and the language model to the Hexagon NPU. EdgeFit Coach reports 2-3x NPU speed-up over CPU for the pose model (its LLM ran on the CPU). Medly runs Qwen on QNN / Hexagon. All three are Snapdragon X laptops; no phone-NPU winner was verified.

3. **Small, named models, sized to the device.** Llama 3.2 3B (GameSense, emerGen, MyStoryPal), Gemma 3n and Gemma 4 E2B / E4B (most Gemma winners), Moondream 0.5B and 2B on a Galaxy S24 (HorizonX), Qwen3 4B (File Fairy). 7B models appear only on Snapdragon X laptops, and so do the Llama 3.2 3B and Qwen3 4B examples here. The models verified running on phones are Gemma 3n, Gemma 4 E2B and Moondream 0.5B / 2B. Larger local models also won on PCs (PenguinAgent and Project Rosie use Gemma 4 26B).

4. **A pipeline of small specialists instead of one large model.** E.M.Pilot (YOLO + EasyOCR + Nomic embeddings + Qwen), emerGen (Whisper + vector DB + Llama), Medly (biomedical NER + Qwen), FieldScreen AI (MedGemma + HeAR + MedASR + TranslateGemma), Sixth Sense (YOLO-NAS pre-filter + Gemma 3n).

5. **Memory and latency management is described as a feature.** KawanIsyarat loads Whisper only when needed and unloads it; GridVeda runs 5+ models on 25 W; Lucid Voice and KawanIsyarat both turn off reasoning / thinking tokens for speed; diffuji and HorizonX document RAM limits.

6. **On-device metrics are reported.** Tokens per second (PreVillage 7.5), fps and latency (EdgeFit, Shepherd), WER (Gem-Care), watts (GridVeda), accuracy (pFOG, GridVeda).

7. **A non-LLM guardrail checks the model (two solid examples).** Gilbeot checks position against the GPS route polyline and suppresses guidance when off-route, and decides left/right from arrow coordinates in code rather than trusting the model's words (confirmed in its Kaggle writeup); Project Rosie uses deterministic templates. Weaker, related examples: Acuífero·Vigía keeps an auditable reasoning trail (a log, not a check); BridgeDx and ClinicDx anchor answers in WHO/MSF guidelines (retrieval grounding, not a non-LLM check).

8. **Hybrid is accepted when the local part is the core.** Qualcomm's rule is that most components must run on the edge. Shepherd keeps the safety loop on the phone and uses cloud for routing, geocoding and its voice assistant. Among Qualcomm winners, emerGen "can also leverage online resources" and EdgeFit Coach's LLM handler can route to lightweight APIs. (Theracat was listed here, but its classifier runs on a laptop host, not on the device.)

9. **Runs on ordinary or mid-range hardware.** KawanIsyarat (mid-range Android), Trido (budget laptops), LENTERA (low-cost microserver), PreVillage and Acuífero·Vigía (Raspberry Pi 5), Gilbeot (tested down to a 2019 Galaxy S10e on CPU), EdgeFit Coach (webcam only, though on a Copilot+ PC).

10. **The phone is used as a sensor, not held as a screen (thin: two solid examples).** Shepherd (iPhone on the cane for LiDAR), Gemma Vision (phone on the chest, controller in hand). In both, inference runs on the phone. Dragverse uses a phone as a 3D scanner feeding a PC and robot, but that is handheld capture, known from one press article, and where its compute ran is [U]. Other phone-inference winners are held and used through the screen or voice (Gilbeot, KawanIsyarat, HorizonX), so this is an option some winners took, not a shared trait.

11. **More than one device working together.** Dragverse (phone + PC + robot), GameSense (PC + Quest headset), Shepherd (phone + ESP32), Theracat (wristband + plush). Qualcomm built a whole hackathon series (Multiverse) around this.

12. **On-device RAG can win when the corpus is private or the setting is offline.** File Fairy (the blog calls it "RAG-based" file search), emerGen (vector DB of manuals plus a chat Q&A), PreVillage (local RAG), PenguinAgent (local RAG over papers), ClinicDx (guideline retrieval). E.M.Pilot uses embeddings for classification and search; calling it RAG is a stretch. Cloud knowledge-retrieval products also won elsewhere (OrgLens, a knowledge-graph expert finder, 1st at LlamaCon; Peel's Elasticsearch evidence engine at HackMIT 2026), so the file has no verified basis for a general "RAG loses" claim; see the softened anti-pattern 1.

## Anti-patterns

Evidence is thinner here because events publish winners, not losers. Each item says what the evidence is.

1. **An undifferentiated assistant or chatbot with no specific user or context (softened by the reviewer; the evidence does not show that chat or RAG products lose).** Verified winners include chat, assistant, agent and retrieval products: FaceTimeOS (a general Mac agent, Cal Hacks 12.0 1st Overall), OrgLens (LlamaCon 1st), CivicFix (a WhatsApp bot, 2nd at the Reskilll Llama hackathon), CurePharma AI and Aarogya Assist (WhatsApp assistants), emerGen (vector DB plus chat Q&A), E.M.Pilot (conversational email), Minerva (avatar tutor), Burrow (browser study companion), HackOverflow (agent knowledge base), PreVillage, BridgeDx and ClinicDx (retrieval over guidelines or services). What they share is a specific user, channel or setting, not the absence of chat. The original evidence for this item is weak: the first page of the HackMIT 2025 gallery lists a text-based "AI executive assistant", an "AI-powered Dropbox", an LLM-to-OS "orchestrating layer" and a model-matching site; none appear among the three HackMIT 2025 winners I could verify, which were a face-controlled game mod, a physical battery micro-grid and a claims generator (https://plume.hackmit.org/gallery?hackathon_id=hack-2025&page=1). Caveat: I could not get the full HackMIT 2025 winner list, so those four projects may or may not have won something, and one of the three known winners (Kava) is itself a documents-in, LLM-output product. A hackathon judge writes that some of the weakest projects bolt a chatbot onto a problem that needed a form (https://dev.to/pranjulrathour/judging-ai-hackathon-projects-what-to-check-when-every-team-says-we-used-ai-19mb, 6 Sept 2026).

2. **Developer-facing agent tooling tends to win sponsor tracks; agent projects win overall when they have a novel interface (weak, softened).** Cal Hacks 12.0's winner-flagged list has many agent tools (Krates, Brydge, Relay, Agent Santa, PointerAI; names confirmed in the gallery, their specific prizes [U]), while 1st and 2nd overall and Most Creative went to FaceTimeOS, Ted.AI and Bob (https://cal-hacks-12-0.devpost.com/project-gallery). Note that FaceTimeOS is itself a multi-agent computer-use project, so "agents rarely win overall" is contradicted by the one overall winner checked; its difference was the FaceTime interface. HackOverflow (agent knowledge commons) fits the sponsor-track pattern at TreeHacks 2026. The Berkeley AI Hackathon 2026 claim (many multi-agent winners in sponsor categories) was not re-checked: [U]. The two track grand prizes checked there went to Lucid Voice and Theracat.

3. **Cloud as the core path in an edge contest.** Qualcomm's Multiverse rules require that "most components must run on the edge" and explicitly allow hybrid if the majority is local; the repo must run from instructions. Some cloud use did not stop winners (emerGen can use online resources; EdgeFit Coach's LLM handler can route to APIs), so the anti-pattern is cloud-dependent core inference, not any cloud call. The Gemma criteria ask whether the tech is real and "not just faked for the demo".

4. **Hard-coded or unverifiable demos.** The dev.to judge checks for this by giving unexpected inputs and asking for a log line or network trace. Teams that cannot show real inference lose credibility. Reviewer caveat: this is one judge's practice, and a verified winner did the opposite of the purist reading. The FaceTimeOS author (Cal Hacks 12.0 1st Overall) advises always having "a hardcoded fallback for when things stop working" and says he removed the agent's clicking for the demo because it was unreliable (https://blog.dylanlu.com/cal-hacks-12/). The supportable version: a demo that is only scripted is a risk where judges probe live; a constrained or fallback demo path did not prevent a win at an expo-style event.

5. **Accuracy claims with no test set.** Same source lists "95% accuracy with no test set" as a red flag. Some winners that quote numbers also state the baseline (Gem-Care), the validation method (GridVeda, 5-fold cross-validation) or the hardware (PreVillage). Not all do: pFOG won a track grand prize quoting ">90% accuracy" with no test set described on its Devpost, so this is a judge's red flag, not something shown to have cost a team a prize.

6. **No answer to "what happens when the model is wrong?"** Same source: "The AI handles it" is a weak answer; thresholds, fallbacks and hand-offs are strong ones. Winners with guardrails are listed in edge trait 7.

7. **Leading with the technology instead of the problem; scope creep.** JetBrains judging notes (June 2026): losing teams skip the problem, start with the stack, attempt five half-features and overrun the demo slot.

8. **Leaving the demo to the last minute.** The TreeHacks 2026 education-track winner nearly lost their demo five minutes before the deadline; the FaceTimeOS team left the venue believing they had not advanced. Both won, but both accounts treat demo readiness as the main risk.

9. **Edge theme entered, but edge not essential.** At Reskilll's Llama hackathon the "AI on Edge" theme produced none of the three placed teams; winners came from Societal Good and WhatsApp themes with clear Indian-language users (https://ai.meta.com/blog/llama-hackathon-india/). Inference: an edge label without a user reason did not outrank a clear user story. This is my reading, not a stated judge comment.

10. **Shortlisted-but-not-awarded in Korea:** six finalists did not win: a secure messenger, a tone-sensitive writing assistant, a screen-privacy tool, a family voice assistant with shared memories, an itinerary planner and a window-arrangement optimiser; the five winners each had a concrete daily task and a multi-model local pipeline (https://www.qualcomm.com/developer/blog/2026/02/on-device-ai-developers-korea). Weak evidence; no judge reasoning is given.

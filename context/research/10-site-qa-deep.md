## What would make this fail (facts only)

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | No Indian code gives a pass mark for hollow tiles; the CPWD wording is "tap and redo what sounds hollow", so a measured percentage has nothing statutory to be judged against. | rows 3.1-3.4 |
| F2 | No Indian rule requires an image record or measured check of reinforcement before a pour. The Japanese category followed a ministry guideline (July 2023); India has no equivalent. | rows 1.1-1.14, 4.6 |
| F3 | The 2025-26 Indian events found are about old or illegal buildings (Delhi audits, Babusapalya, Karnataka clause 31). None of the inquiries read attributes a collapse to a bar-count, spacing or diameter error, and none creates a pre-pour duty. | rows 1.2, 1.4, 1.7 |
| F4 | The Karnataka Bill is passed but not in force, and contains no handover-inspection mandate; the inspection firm had to ask for one. | rows 1.1, 1.3 |
| F5 | MahaRERA's annual quality certificate is signed by the project's own site engineer and promoter; it does not require an independent measurement. | row 1.10 [U] |
| F6 | The inspection market is small in rupees: about Rs 8,000-13,000 per flat, and 1% of Bengaluru's 2026 completions is about 690 inspections. Leading firms are unfunded or have no disclosed funding. | rows 2.3, 2.6, 2.7 |
| F7 | Inspection firms already have their own snag software and 360-degree reports; the tap map would be a feature inside their tool, not a replacement for it. | row 5.6 |
| F8 | Published tap-sound accuracy falls from 98.5% in the lab to 91.5% in the field and 82% with 75 dB speech; an older field figure is 81%. Construction floors are noisy and the fa-16 kill line is 90%. | rows 4.1, 4.2 [U] |
| F9 | No published test on a stock phone microphone or on vitrified tile over a thick cement-sand bed, which is the common Indian floor. | rows 4.3, 3.5 |
| F10 | MLIT expects bar diameter to be judged correctly as a nominal size and slab spacing within about 5 mm. A single phone camera with one marker sits at the edge of the spacing target and is marginal on 2 mm diameter steps. | rows 4.6, 4.7, 4.10, 4.11 |
| F11 | The only shipping phone-plus-marker system (Tokyu) needs five angled photos and server-side processing, even for one layer. Fully on-device, single-photo operation is not demonstrated by any product found. | row 4.8 |
| F12 | Lower-layer bars, cover and lap length cannot be measured to tolerance without depth hardware or multiple views; those are the items a pour card also has to cover. | row 4.12 |
| F13 | The pour card is signed by the contractor's and the client's engineers together; no evidence found that Indian PMCs or QA firms pay per pour for tooling, and advertised third-party QA fees are capped near 1% of project cost. | rows 3.6, 2.8 |

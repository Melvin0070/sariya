Research date: 2026-10-03. Research only, no product ideas.

Conventions used in this file:

- **[U]** = unverified: no source found, or the number was not measured on the event phone / its exact SoC build.
- **(snippet)** = the number was seen in a search-result summary of the cited URL; the page itself was not opened. Treat as weaker than an opened page.
- **8EG5-Galaxy** = "Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy", the device label Qualcomm AI Hub uses for its measurements. The iQOO 15 uses the standard SM8850, so every AI Hub number is **[U] for the event phone** (same SoC family, different OEM build, thermals and RAM).
- "tok/s" is decode speed unless stated. TTFT = time to first token.
- Qualcomm AI Hub LLM runtimes: `GENIE` = Qualcomm Genie on QNN/HTP (NPU). `GENIEX_LLAMACPP` = Qualcomm's GenieX wrapper around llama.cpp with GGUF; the model cards do not say which compute unit it uses, so NPU use for those rows is **[U]**.

**Do not confuse the two "15" phones.** iQOO 15 = Snapdragon 8 **Elite** Gen 5 (SM8850). iQOO 15R = Snapdragon 8 Gen 5, a different, lower-tier chip. Chipset-compiled NPU binaries (QNN context binaries, per-SoC `.litertlm` files) built for one will not load on the other, and AI Hub numbers quoted in this file are for the 8 Elite Gen 5 only.

**Conclusion.** The Finale loaner is most likely the **iQOO 15 (Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5, SM8850)**. It is the only "flagship iQOO phone" on sale in India on OriginOS 6 as of 3 Oct 2026, and Reskilll and iQOO both name it. Residual risk: the Finale could use a different unit (iQOO 13 = Snapdragon 8 Elite; iQOO 15R = Snapdragon 8 Gen 5; a pre-release iQOO 16 = 8 Elite Gen 6). RAM variant (12 vs 16 GB) is **[U]**.

### 1.0 A catalogue listing does not prove NPU execution

AI Hub now mixes two kinds of asset under one catalogue:

- **Chipset-compiled assets**: QNN context binaries / QAIRT bundles (runtimes `GENIE`, `GENIEX_QAIRT`, `QNN_CONTEXT_BINARY`, `PRECOMPILED_QNN_ONNX`, `QNN_DLC`), quantised w4a16 or w8a16. These target the Hexagon NPU.
- **Portable GGUF files** labelled "Universal" (runtime `GENIEX_LLAMACPP`, q4_0 or q8_0). These are ordinary llama.cpp files. llama.cpp can run them on CPU, on the Adreno GPU via OpenCL, or on the NPU through its experimental Hexagon backend. The model cards do not say which was used. Several cards show three unlabelled rows per device and context with different speeds (for example Qwen3.5-0.8B at 80.9 / 62.0 / 45.7 tok/s), which is consistent with three different backends, but the cards do not label them: **[U]**.

Bottom line of the physics check:
- With **no** scale reference, the only options are a learned prior (10-25%, sometimes much worse) or IMU-based VIO (1-5% after motion). The VIO route normally means ARCore, and the iQOO 15 is not on Google's ARCore supported-devices list (checked 2026-10-03), so it must be tested on the phone before relying on it.
- All error bands in the table above are the original author's estimates (benchmark numbers plus geometry), not phone measurements. The reviewer re-did the stereo arithmetic (dZ = Z^2 x 0.5 / (1500 x 0.012 m): 0.25 cm, 2.8 cm, 25 cm, 69 cm at 0.3 / 1 / 3 / 5 m) and the 300 px reference figure (4 px / 300 px = 1.3%); both are arithmetically correct, but B, f and the disparity error are assumed, not measured.
- With a coplanar known-size reference, a plain photo can reach 1-3% without any neural network.
- The depth models that are precompiled for this NPU (DA V2 Small, da3-small) output **relative** depth. They do not give metres by themselves.
- The metric mono models with permissive licences are Metric3D v2 (BSD-2) and DA3Metric-Large (Apache-2.0); MoGe-2 has MIT code and MIT-tagged weights on Hugging Face. DA3-Small and DA3-Base (up-to-scale, not metric) are Apache-2.0; DA3-Large / Giant are CC-BY-NC. MoGe-2 ViT-S (35M) is the only metric model small enough to resemble the NPU-precompiled depth models. None has a published Snapdragon phone speed: **[U]**. Depth Pro, UniDepth, MASt3R and most DA Base/Large checkpoints are non-commercial.

### 6.2 Most likely match

**`NandhaKishorM/laya` (Convai Innovations) is the most likely match.** Reasons:

1. It is by far the largest "Laya" repo (30k stars in two weeks; released 19 Sep 2026) and its Hugging Face model `convaiinnovations/laya` was on the HF trending list on 30 Sep 2026 with ~4.5k likes (third-party digest: https://github.com/stevenko2002/agents-radar/issues/1528).
2. It is an Indian, open-source (Apache-2.0), local-first model, which fits the hackathon's "local or open-source model at the core" bonus. Founder Nandakishor Mukkunnoth, Kasaragod, Kerala (https://www.crunchbase.com/person/nandakishor-m ; https://startupfortune.com/open-source-is-pulling-away-from-jev-laya-mukkunnoth/, snippets).
3. It is small enough for a phone (322-421M encoder) and already has community ports: Snapdragon **X laptop** NPU (not phone), Android **CPU** via ONNX Runtime, and Core ML. No port runs it on a Snapdragon phone NPU.
4. People refer to it as "the Laya GitHub" because the GitHub repo, not a paper or product page, is its main home.

Uncertainty: the user gave no context. No source ties Laya to iQOO, Reskilll, Sarvam or AI4Bharat; none of the Reskilll pages fetched mention it. If the user meant something speech- or music-related, the nearest alternatives are `ysharma3501/LayaCodec` (audio codec) and `aayushch/laya` (local notification assistant). Confidence that `NandhaKishorM/laya` is the intended repo: high but not certain, **[U]** until the user confirms.

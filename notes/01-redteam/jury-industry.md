# Sariya red team: industry and market jury (hostile)

Lane: the construction industry and the market only (not the hackathon jury, not camera physics). 30 Sep 2026.
Method: 41 web searches, using search-result summaries only (no page fetches). Tags:
- **[S]**: seen in a search result (URL given; re-open it before it goes on a slide);
- **[B]**: my background belief;
- **[I]**: my inference.

The in-character quotes are simulated opinions, not evidence. The evidence is in each "step out" paragraph.

---

## 0. Bottom line

**Kill-shot.** No one is obliged to stop a pour, so Sariya is a voluntary check, and the spec gives the phone to the mason, the one person the check incriminates.
- Voluntary checks get adopted by careful owners, who already pay an engineer or get a free brand technical-service visit.
- They get skipped by the owners who actually kill people. The spec's own hero case, Babusapalya, was a G+6 built for two years without approvals, and the owner kept building after BBMP issued three stop-work notices [S, §7].
- The "pour-permit QR" permits nothing. No bank, municipality, concrete supplier or PMAY-G officer asks for one.

**Verdict: WOUNDED.**
- **Dead as written:** the market story in IDEA §3.1-3.3 (the mason as operator, the pour permit, PMAY-G and lenders as payers, and brands that "want a reason to be on site").
- **Survives:** a narrower product. It is an instrument for the people who are already paid to stand at the pour: steel and cement brands' technical-service engineers, organised builders' QC supervisors, and the owner's engineer. The mason becomes the listener.

**Top payer:** steel brands (Tata Tiscon, JSW Neosteel), which would fund it as a field-force productivity, brand-verification and loyalty tool. Cement brands come second.

**Most important alternative:** the free brand technical-service visit at slab casting. Ambuja reported slab-casting guidance and supervision at **31,698 customer sites** in one year [S].

## 1. The panel

### 1.1 Practising structural engineer, Bengaluru (designs and signs G+2/G+3 houses)

> *(simulated)* "At pre-pour I find **location** errors, not millimetre errors:
> - cover blocks missing, so the mesh sits on the shuttering;
> - no chairs, so the top steel gets trampled flat;
> - cantilever (chajja) bars laid at the bottom;
> - top bars over the supports missing;
> - laps bunched at one section;
> - beam bars not anchored into the end column;
> - column ties stopping short of the joint;
> - a 10 mm bar substituted when the 12s ran out.
>
> Uniform 150-200 mm stirrups and 90° hooks, yes. But this is zone II, and I won't call a 90° hook on my own drawing a code failure.
>
> Approve a pour from a phone pack? **Never.** If it falls, the police arrest the engineer first. I'd use it to pick which of my eight sites to visit today, and to keep a record. A visit costs me half a day in traffic, and many owners buy only the drawings and never call me for the steel."

**Step out.**
- **Fees.**
  - Bengaluru structural-design packages cost about **₹7/sq ft** for 500-2,000 sq ft [S](https://sqft.expert/structural-consultants-bangalore).
  - Site visits are quoted at **₹5,000-30,000 per visit**, or ₹5,000-20,000 a day [S](https://aecord.com/blog/structural-engineering-fees-india-residential). That source is a blog, and small Bengaluru practices probably charge less [B].
  - So a G+2 on a 30×40 plot buys its structural design for roughly ₹20-25k [I], and paid pre-pour visits are an add-on many owners skip [I].
- **Liability is real.** After the Taratala (Kolkata) under-construction warehouse collapse in June 2026, a structural engineer and a supervisor were arrested, and a municipal sub-assistant engineer followed in Sep 2026 [S](https://thecsrjournal.in/kolkata-warehouse-collapse-leads-arrests-engineer-supervisor), [S](https://www.thehawk.in/news/india/kolkata-civic-engineer-held-in-taratala-warehouse-collapse-case-total-arrests-rise-to-7).
  - Engineers will refuse a remote "approve" click, or bury it in disclaimers [I].
  - The realistic loop is **triage plus documentation**, not remote approval.
- **What Sariya measures versus what kills.**
  - The spec checks count, spacing, diameter class, stirrup zones, hooks and whether cover blocks are present.
  - It does not address cantilever top steel, top/bottom placement, laps, anchorage, joint ties or chairs [I from IDEA §3.3].
  - A construction-literate juror asks about the cantilever first [B].
- **Zone II.** IS 13920 is written for seismic zones III-V [B; the rulebook stream must verify the scope clause]. In the Finale city, "FAIL: 90° hook" can contradict a valid drawing. The rulebook must defer to the drawing and mark seismic detailing as *advisory* where the code doesn't apply.
- **The best hook for engineers is Nambike Nakshe.**
  - BBMP-registered architects and engineers self-certify plans for up to 4 units on plots up to 50×80 ft, with deemed approval in 15 days. BBMP still inspects [S](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/karnataka-launches-online-building-plan-approval-process-in-bbmp-limits-124090201037_1.html), [S](https://www.gktoday.in/what-is-nambike-nakshe-scheme/).
  - Self-certification shifts risk onto the certifying professional, which makes a signed construction record worth more to them [I].

### 1.2 Mason-contractor (maistry) running 3-4 sites

> *(simulated)* "Labour contract: the owner buys the steel and I get ₹230-350 a square foot. Why would I skimp steel that isn't mine? What I save is **time**: stirrups at 150 instead of 100 means a third less tying, a 90° hook is quicker to bend, and cover blocks walk off.
>
> The slab gang is booked for 7 a.m. If your phone tells me at 6:30 to add four stirrups in every beam, who pays the gang to wait? And you want me to GPS-sign my own mistakes for the owner's engineer? No.
>
> My phone is a ₹10,000 Redmi. I'd use it for brand points like the bag coupons, if a green result gets my stage bill paid faster, and if it runs the evening before. 'Work passport'? I get work because my last owner tells his brother-in-law."

**Step out.**
- **Contract form.** Labour-only contracts, where the owner supplies materials, run **₹230-350/sq ft** in Bengaluru, and masons earn ₹600-800 a day [S](https://architects4design.com/labor-contractor-rates/), [S](https://aecord.com/blog/labour-rates-for-house-construction-india-2026).
  - Under labour-only contracts, the maistry's incentive is to save labour: fewer stirrups, faster hooks, skipped cover blocks.
  - Under item-rate or turnkey contracts, he also saves steel [I].
  - Either way, **he is the party a FAIL costs**, so he will not volunteer a signed record of his own shortfalls [I].
- **Timing conflict.** A FAIL on pour morning costs a gang-day. The scan belongs after binding, the evening before [I].
- **Loyalty rails exist, but they pay per purchase, not per quality.**
  - Ambuja Abhimaan: 1.2 lakh+ key contractors [S](https://www.constructionworld.in/cement-news/ambuja-cements--loyalty-programme-wins-top-award/41030).
  - UltraTech Utec Pass (billed as the largest programme) [S](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ultratech.rewardsloyalty&hl=en_IN).
  - JSW Privilege Club for masons, contractors and retailers [S](https://www.jswneosteel.in/privilege-club).
  - Points per third-party-verified pour is a plausible bribe. The "work passport" has no evidence behind it, since IHB hiring runs on referrals [B].
- **Device.** No searches turned up India-specific data on masons' phones. My belief is ₹8-15k Androids [B], which can't run a flagship-NPU pipeline [I].
- **Conclusion.** The incentive problem kills "the mason sweeps". It doesn't kill "the mason hears the fix in Kannada".

### 1.3 Homeowner: G+2 on the Bengaluru outskirts, ₹60-90 lakh, no engineer on site

> *(simulated)* "I'm on site on Sundays and don't know what to check in steel. I trust my cousin's maistry, the brand rolled on the bars, and an engineer friend who looks at my WhatsApp photos. ₹2-5k a slab is nothing on a ₹75 lakh house, but I want **a person who signs**, not an app that says 're-scan'. Free from Tata or UltraTech, I'd take it. Brick&Bolt pitched me 470 quality checks."

**Step out.**
- **Willingness-to-pay anchors.**
  - Bengaluru home inspections cost **₹6,000-20,000** per home, or about ₹25/sq ft [S](https://propcheckup.com/home-inspection-bangalore/), [S](https://www.deccanclap.com/projects/4144/best-home-inspection-service-in-bangalore), [S](https://www.snageasy.in/home-inspection-bangalore).
  - Nemmadi claims **12,000+ inspections** across 12+ cities [S](https://nemmadi.in/about-us/). Its products are pre-possession and resale snagging [S](https://nemmadi.in/home-inspection/). "Multi-stage" inspection of homes under construction is offered, but no price is visible [S](https://nemmadi.in/).
  - **Hostile reading:** Indian home inspection grew where buyers can *see* defects, not at the pour [I].
- **Organised builders already sell verified quality.**
  - Brick&Bolt runs its QASCON framework with **470+ checks**. An independent supervisor signs off each milestone, contractors are paid only after the stage is verified, and the owner app shows stage reports and photos [S](https://www.bricknbolt.com/construction-company-bengaluru).
  - It has raised about $24M, reports ₹50-100 Cr revenue in FY25, and claims 10,000+ homes [S](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/brick-bolt/__RC03ZTNPrLOFukk8vCk0BREwWbQSgfUqUw2Yw4wQCdc).
  - Local design-build firms sell a "dedicated site engineer" plus daily WhatsApp updates [S](https://www.buildhood.com/), [S](https://100pillars.in/).
- **Role.** The homeowner has the incentive and the money but is rarely at the pour [B], so they are the **payer and beneficiary, not the operator**.
- **Adverse selection.** Babusapalya-type owners (§7) are not this persona [I].

### 1.4 Head of a steel or cement brand's IHB programme

> *(simulated, a composite)* "We are **already at the pour**. Our engineers supervise slab castings for free, and we sell factory-bent 135° stirrups and cut-and-bend steel so the bar-bender can't get the hook wrong.
>
> My KPIs are tonnes or bags per IHB site, share of slab-stage purchases, active influencers in the loyalty app, visits per technical engineer, and complaints.
>
> I'd fund you only if:
> - my engineers cover 3-5× the sites;
> - my brand is verified on site (rib marks against the invoice);
> - I get the slab date 48 h ahead;
> - the homeowner gets a report with my logo;
> - nothing you produce says *my* steel failed.
>
> It lives inside Aashiyana, Utec or the Privilege Club, white-labelled, and my IT vendor will want your source."

**Step out.**
- **Evidence.**
  - Ambuja: slab casting at 31,698 sites [S](https://www.ambujacement.com/annual-reports2022/relationship-capital.html).
  - UltraTech: Mobile Concrete Testing Lab ("technical assistance during concreting by a qualified … Civil Engineer", no extra cost) and Counter Meets for IHBs [S](https://www.ultratechcement.com/technical-service). Its Utec platform connects owners to engineers and contractors [S](https://www.ultratechcement.com/utecbyultratech).
  - Tata Tiscon: site supervisory services, 100+ customer-service engineers [S](https://tatatiscon.co.in/home-builders/home-building-step-step.aspx).
  - Superlinks: 135° hook with 65 mm extension, "70% faster" [S](https://tatatiscon.co.in/ultima-stirrups-superlinks).
  - ReadyBuild: cut-and-bend, couplers, a central bar-bending-schedule service, 10 facilities, 6-40 mm [S](https://readybuild.tatasteel.com/content/dam/tatasteelltdaemprogram/readybuild/brochure/Cut-Bend-Rebars-Brochure.pdf).
  - JSW Neosteel: on-site support, testing and mason training [S](https://www.jswneosteel.in/solutions). JSW Eklavya academy targets 20,000+ fabricators, masons and contractors, including bar bending [S](https://www.jswsteel.in/jsw-eklavya-skill-academy).
- **IDEA §3.2 is wrong** that brands "want a reason to be on site at the pour". They are there already, with a free human. Sariya is an upgrade to an existing service, not a way in [I].
- **Incentive fit.**
  - *Steel brand:* every "missing bars" FAIL sells steel, and rib-mark OCR verifies its brand against counterfeits. That aligns [I].
  - *Cement brand:* a steel check sells no cement, but it turns the slab-casting visit into a branded, signed report for the homeowner [I].
  - *Hidden KPI:* the slab date is a sales event (a slab consumes cement by the hundred-bag lot [B]). A tool that logs pours 24-48 h ahead is lead generation [I].
- **Risks.** An in-house clone, procurement through an IT vendor, legal fear of issuing a "PASS", and a slow cycle [B].

### 1.5 Construction-loan technical manager (HFC or bank)

> *(simulated)* "Our valuer checks **stage and percentage complete** against the plan and the value of our security. We never check reinforcement; we don't want structural liability. A tamper-evident, geotagged stage pack that replaces a visit interests me: it cuts cost and fake-progress fraud. Steel QA? Only if you can show it lowers default, and you can't."

**Step out.**
- **Tranches.** Typical splits are foundation 15-25%, plinth/column 20-25%, lintel/roof 25-30%, and brickwork/plaster 15-20% [S](https://www.aavas.in/blog/home-construction-loan-disbursement-process). A technical valuer or field officer inspects before each release [S](https://www.nobroker.in/home-loan/sbi-home-loan-process/).
- **Price anchor.** Aptus's terms (MITC) charge **₹1,500 + GST for the first technical visit (loans up to ₹10 lakh) and ₹750 + GST for each later one** [S](https://aptusindia.s3.amazonaws.com/Aptus+HFC+MITC+(English).pdf). That is the ceiling for a lender-side per-verification fee, and it is the borrower's money [I].
- **Segment.** Affordable HFCs (Aavas, Aptus, India Shelter) lend for self-construction to informal-income borrowers in semi-urban and rural India [S](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AAVAS.NS/profile/). The geography is right, but the product they buy is progress verification.
- **Conclusion.** Lenders are a buyer for Sariya's **evidence layer** (signed, geotagged stage packs), not for its rulebook [I].

### 1.6 PMAY-G block engineer or state rural-housing official

> *(simulated)* "Our houses are 25 m² minimum, mostly load-bearing brick masonry with an **RCC roof slab and lintel band**, built by the village mason. Our officials upload the stage photos. My problem isn't hooks; it's whether the house **exists**. I have a couple of technical assistants for thousands of houses. A steel check fits only if it replaces a visit and lives inside AwaasApp, and NIC owns AwaasApp."

**Step out.**
- **Process.**
  - There are seven stages and at least three instalments. The first is paid at sanction, the second is mapped to foundation or plinth, and the third to windowsill, lintel or roof cast [S](https://rh.odisha.gov.in/guidelines/PMAY(G)_guidelines.pdf), [S](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2025/Full_PA_PMAY-ReportNo-8_English_05-15-2025-signed-0694bb6ecc93c28.69875526.pdf).
  - The "roof cast" photo is taken *after* the pour. The pre-pour moment is not a stage [I].
- **CAG (UP audit, tabled Dec 2025).** Joint physical verification of 2,079 houses marked "completed" in the MIS found **77** complete. Trained masons were not mapped to individual houses, and demonstration houses weren't built to the recommended designs [S](https://theaidem.com/en-cag-reports-on-up-and-chhattisgarh-failed-pmay-scheme-delivery-promise/) (secondary source). The institution can't yet verify *existence*, so steel geometry is far down its list [I].
- **Steel content.** Typically a roof slab plus bands [B]. Design menus follow local typologies [S](https://cbri.res.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/pahal.pdf). Sariya's Tier 1 planar-mesh check fits the roof slab; cages and 135° hooks mostly don't apply [I].
- **Budget.** Unit assistance is about ₹1.2 lakh [B], which leaves no per-house budget [I].
- **Conclusion.** PMAY-G is an impact footnote or a state pilot, **not a payer**.

**Does the incentive problem kill "mason sweeps the phone"?** For the **operator** role, yes; for the **listener** role, no. (§1.2 has the evidence.)
- The mason is paid by area or by day and is the party a FAIL costs. He will not generate signed evidence against himself, and nobody can force him to.
- **Recommended operators:**
  1. the brand's technical-service engineer, who is already on site and gets 3-5× the coverage;
  2. the organised builder's QC supervisor;
  3. the owner's engineer or an inspection firm, for engaged owners.
- The mason hears the fix by voice and earns loyalty points on a third-party-verified PASS.
- Keep the mason as the persona who *hears* the fix; never claim masons will self-inspect. Whether that still counts as a "Community App" is for the jury lane to judge.

| Lens | Score | One-line reason |
|---|---:|---|

*Feasibility is left unchanged; physics is another lane.

- **Pain:** severe but unevidenced.

## 6. The five changes that most strengthen the market story

1. **User: the person with the incentive sweeps, and the mason hears.**
   - Make the brand technical-service engineer (or the builder's QC supervisor, or the owner's engineer) the operator.
   - *Why:* it removes the self-incrimination objection every construction-literate juror will raise. It also attaches Sariya to a behaviour that already happens at scale (31,698 Ambuja slab-casting visits).
   - The demo stays the same: a "technical engineer" sweeps and the mason hears the fix in Kannada.
2. **Payer: steel brand first, with KPIs it already tracks.**
   - Pitch sites covered per technical engineer per day, and "every FAIL for missing bars = tonnes sold".
   - Add rib-mark OCR as brand verification against the invoice, and slab-date leads 24-48 h ahead.
   - For cement brands, pitch a branded, signed "slab report" handed to the owner.
   - *Why:* it answers "who pays" with an existing budget line, and aligns incentives, because a steel brand profits from a FAIL.
3. **Framing and feature: drop the "pour-permit QR" and gate money that already moves at stages.**
   - Attach the pack to money that is already gated: the maistry's stage bill, organised builders' verified-stage payouts (Brick&Bolt does exactly this), and HFC tranches (replace a ₹750-1,500 visit).
   - Move the scan to **the evening before the pour**.
   - Demote PMAY-G to an impact footnote (roof-slab mesh only).
   - *Why:* a permit with no enforcement point is the first thing an industry juror punctures, and pour-morning FAILs are commercially toxic.
4. **Evidence: replace anecdote with a measured defect rate.**
   - At the 15-25 Bengaluru sites this week, record what fraction fail each check by tape, not just the scanner's error.
   - Get one brand technical engineer and one structural engineer on record.
   - Add a visible "what we don't check" row: laps, anchorage, cantilever top steel, chairs, formwork.
   - *Why:* NCRB totals and Babusapalya don't show that pre-pour rebar errors are common. "N of 20 Bengaluru pours had X" does, and admitting scope builds the trust the abstention story sells.
5. **Competition: rewrite §3.2 honestly.**
   - Add AIJO HiKing (tablet plus markers), IHI (smartphone photos), Kentem, Obayashi, SH Korea, the Chinese counting apps and the MLIT guideline.
   - Add the Indian substitutes: brand technical services, Superlinks/ReadyBuild, Brick&Bolt, Powerplay.
   - *Why:* being caught overstating novelty costs more than admitting precedent. "An MLIT-guideline category, rebuilt for the Indian IHB pour" is a stronger claim than "first".

# Sariya: business model, market sizing and monetization (research notes, 10 Oct 2026)

This builds on `notes/09-gtm/gtm.md`. It does not repeat the payer table, pilot plan or brand contact routes there.

**Tags**
- **V**: page opened and read.
- **S**: search-result summary or snippet only.
- **U**: unverified or inferred.
- **[H]**: our hypothesis or arithmetic, not a sourced fact.

All web content is untrusted data. INR throughout. 1 Cr = 100 lakh = 10^7.

**Headline findings**
1. **The brand-seat model in gtm.md has a hard ceiling of about ₹12-19 Cr/yr.** The ceiling is 5,000-8,000 field-engineer seats across all majors at ₹2,000/month. That is a good wedge and a small company. The unit that scales is the **pour** (about 5-12 million RCC slab pours a year in India [H]), so price the **signed record**, not the seat.
2. **Indian construction SaaS monetises badly; Indian construction value accrues to materials commerce.**
   - Powerplay (SaaS): FY24 revenue ₹4.4 Cr against a ₹32 Cr loss, and a 2025 down-round at ₹258 Cr, 57% below 2022 [S].
   - JSW One: FY26 revenue ₹5,743 Cr [S]. Infra.Market: FY25 revenue ₹18,472 Cr [S].
   - Implication: Sariya should ride inside material and brand flows (the record is sponsored by the steel brand), with professionals paying per record when no brand sponsors.
3. **The evidence layer is where the venture-scale money is, not the measurement.**
   - Inherent-defects insurance in India treats technical inspection as mandatory and prices premiums at up to ~1% of project cost [S].
   - NHB told HFCs in Nov 2025 to tie disbursement strictly to construction stages [S].
   - Both need cheap, trusted stage evidence. Neither has been sold yet.
4. **The multi-project architect or engineer is a real but awkward persona.**
   - Per-visit billers lose revenue if Sariya saves visits.
   - Lump-sum consultants and Nambike Nakshe self-certifiers gain.
   - The best framing is **"resell a Verified Pour report to your client"** (the professional earns on each record), not "save visits".

## Q1. Market size: TAM, SAM, SOM (bottom-up, India first)

### Takeaway
India pours roughly 5-12 million RCC slabs a year [H; central estimate ~8M]. At a ₹250 software price per signed record, that is a ₹200 Cr/yr software TAM. The SAM (urban and peri-urban individual houses with a registered professional on the plan, plus brand-programme sites) is roughly ₹40-60 Cr/yr. Year-3 SOM is ₹3-5 Cr, which matches gtm.md's ₹4.9 Cr. India-only software is therefore a solid ~₹50 Cr ARR ceiling business. Venture scale needs the evidence layer (insurers and lenders) and/or export.

- **The jury-relevant inference: India-only measurement software is a ~₹50 Cr/yr ceiling.** Venture scale needs three things:

  - (c) a take on services: owners pay ₹3,000-5,000 for a human check, and Sariya-equipped inspectors keep that while Sariya takes 10-20%.

## Q2. Business model options compared, and which to lead with

### Takeaway
Lead with **per-signed-record pricing ("Verified Pour", ₹149-299 per pour)**, paid by whoever already pays for the pour visit:
1. the steel or cement brand, as a sponsored record inside its IHB programme;
2. otherwise the professional (architect, engineer, inspector), who resells it to the owner.

Keep seats as the enterprise wrapper for brand field forces (gtm.md). Treat the lender and insurer evidence layer as the Year-2+ expansion that lifts price per record. A marketplace and PMAY are not leads.

- **Why A leads.**
  1. The unit of value is the pour, and pours are ~1,000× more numerous than brand seats.
  2. A brand-sponsored record looks like a marketing or lead cost to the brand (slab-date leads, rib-mark brand verification, a branded report in the owner's hand). It does not need a "QA budget", which nobody has (gtm.md honest counter).
  3. The same SKU works for professionals without a brand, so there is no channel conflict.
  4. It maps cleanly to a later per-verification fee for lenders and insurers.
- **Why B stays.** It is the beachhead contract and the logo. Price it as "seats include N records/month; overage per record", so the ceiling becomes a floor.

### Inferences
- Freemium for individual engineers converts in India only if the free tier is genuinely useful (live measurement, no signed record) and the paid trigger is external, e.g. a client, brand or lender asking for a signed record. SafetyCulture's model is free use with paid organisational features; Fieldwire's free tier is capped by projects. A Sariya equivalent: unlimited measurement-only mode free; signed and approved records paid [H].
- Per-project fees (₹2,000-5,000 per house covering all slabs) are a good alternative packaging for consultants on lump-sum fees [H]. Test both in pilot C (gtm.md).

## Q3. Pricing benchmarks (beyond gtm.md's WTP anchors)

### Takeaway
Globally, field-QA seats cost $24-39/user/month, about ₹2,000-3,300 [H at ~₹84/$]. India's only visible construction-SaaS price is ₹6,000-10,000/month per contractor company (Powerplay). Human pre-pour checks cost owners ₹1,500-15,000 per visit. **₹199-299 per signed record is 2-20% of the human check it documents and ~0.01% of the slab's construction cost**, an easy "rounding error" price [H].

## Q4. The multi-project architect or engineer persona: willingness to pay and liability

### Takeaway
The persona exists: ~1.23 lakh registered architects, plus structural consultants and Nambike Nakshe self-certifiers. But "saves visits" is a weak pitch for anyone billing per visit. The pitch that should convert is **"protect your licence, and bill your client for a signed Verified Pour report"**: a liability shield plus a resale margin. The price should be per record, or ₹499-999/month for ~10 active sites [H]. No evidence was found that Indian insurers discount professional-indemnity premiums for documented checks; treat that as a pilot question, not a claim.

### Cited findings
- 1,22,971 registered architects (CoA, ~Jun 2023); 85.4% of an earlier 93,117 were 45 or under — [architecture.live](https://architecture.live/changing-demographics-of-the-architectural-profession-in-india-prem-chandavarkar/) [V]
- The CoA scale of charges is a professional norm, not law. A CPWD memo (2015) says it is approved by neither Parliament nor the Central Government. Supervision is by a clerk of works or site supervisor paid by the client, under the architect's direction — [search summary of the CPWD OM](https://architexturez.net/pst/az-cf-176738-1453906556) [S]
- Small-house supervision ₹1,500-2,000 per visit — [houseyog](https://www.houseyog.com/blog/architect-fees-charges-for-house-designing-in-india) [S]
- **Liability is real and rising:**
  - Taratala (Kolkata) warehouse collapse, Jun 2026: police arrested the **structural engineer** and the construction supervisor, later also a KMC sub-assistant engineer (7 arrests); the SIT is examining design, materials and approvals — [The Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/kolkata/kolkata-civic-engineer-held-in-taratala-warehouse-collapse-case-total-arrests-rise-to-7-1121110); [CSR Journal](https://thecsrjournal.in/kolkata-warehouse-collapse-leads-arrests-engineer-supervisor/) [S]
  - Death tolls conflict across sources (5, 9 or 16).
  - Delhi, Satya Niketan, Sep 2026: 7 dead; owner and contractor arrested, no engineer named — [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/india/mahesh-gupta-arrested-as-5th-suspect-in-satya-niketan-collapse/tldr) [S]

| Lump-sum architect or consultant, 5-15 live houses | % fee or fixed fee | Yes: each avoided pre-pour trip saves ~₹1,500-3,000 of time and travel | Remote sign-off plus a client-facing report | ₹499-999/month, or a ₹2,000-5,000 per-project pack |
| Per-visit structural consultant | Per visit | No, it cannibalises visits | Resale: "Verified Pour report", ₹1,000-2,000 to the owner, ₹299 cost | Per record |
| Nambike Nakshe self-certifier | Plan fee | Indirect: liability | A dated, signed record per slab on plans they certified | ₹99-299 per record; ₹299-499/month (gtm.md) |
| Design-build firm QC (Brick&Bolt type) | Internal cost | Yes: fewer senior visits, fewer payout disputes | API or module | Per site or per record, enterprise |

- **The ₹/month logic.** A consultant with 8 active sites at ~1 slab per site per month who replaces one trip in two saves ~₹6,000-12,000/month of time. A ₹999 plan is ~10% of the saving, which is defensible. The saving holds only if the consultant's fee is fixed [H].
- **The insurance angle.** Documented pre-pour checks fit what PI and defect insurers already value. Technical inspection is a mandatory underwriting step for inherent-defects insurance (Q5). No insurer discount for PI was found; the ask is "a premium credit for records-backed practices", tested with one broker in the pilot [H].
- **The flip side (gtm.md risk).** A signed record is also evidence against the signer. Some engineers will refuse to create records. That is why selling through the owner's demand ("my client asks for it") beats selling to the engineer cold.

## Q6. Unit economics

### Takeaway
Software COGS per record is a few rupees. **The cost that decides the margin is human support and rulebook review.** At gtm.md's assumption of 1 reviewer per 2,000 sites/yr it is ~₹140 per pour, which wipes out a ₹249 price. The support ratio must reach about 1 per 20,000 sites, through self-serve onboarding and brand-run first-line support, for 80%+ gross margins [H].

### Cited findings
- gtm.md inputs (all [U]/[H] there): cloud ₹1-2 per site/yr; card ₹15-40 per site; engineer time 8-12 min per member; 1 support or rulebook engineer per 2,000 sites/yr.
- Benchmark loss profiles:
  - Powerplay FY24: ₹4.39 Cr revenue, ₹31.9 Cr loss [S above].
  - BuildNext FY25: ~₹18 Cr operating income, ~₹9 Cr loss [S above].
  - JSW One FY26: ₹106.5 Cr loss on ₹5,743 Cr [S above].

### Inferences: per-record P&L [H]

| Line | Professional record (₹249) | Brand-sponsored record (₹149) |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud, signing, storage | ₹1-2 | ₹1-2 |
| WhatsApp or SMS share to owner, payment fees (~2%) | ₹5-8 | ₹1-3 (invoiced) |
| Card or strip amortised (₹30 / ~3 pours) | ₹10 | ₹10 (brand may print) |
| Support and rulebook at 1:2,000 sites (₹7 lakh/yr ÷ ~5,000 pours) | **₹140** | ₹140 |
| Support at 1:20,000 sites | ₹14 | ₹14 |
| **Gross margin** at 1:2,000 → 1:20,000 | 36% → **87%** | -3% → **83%** |

- **Customer acquisition (CAC) and lifetime value (LTV), professional channel [H].**
  - CAC through ACCE(I), IEI and IAStructE chapter demos and brand dealer meets: ~₹1,500-3,000 per paying professional.
  - At 4 records/month × ₹249 × 85% margin ≈ ₹850/month, payback is 2-4 months.
  - LTV at 30% annual churn ≈ ₹30,000.
  - LTV/CAC ≈ 10×, if 4 records/month holds. Measure it in pilot C.
- **Brand channel [H].** CAC is a 3-6 month enterprise cycle (₹3-5 lakh of founder time and pilot cost) for ₹15-120 lakh ACV, so payback is under 6 months once signed.
- **Engineer-time economics for the brand [H].** At 8-12 min per member and ~4-6 members per slab, a pour takes ~45-60 min of scanning. A brand engineer then does ~5-6 checked pours a day. A single human visit (gtm.md, loaded) costs ₹800-1,500, so the per-record price must stay under ~20% of that, i.e. ₹150-300. That matches the price band.

- No measured support load.

## Q8. Moat and risks (business side; adds to gtm.md §5)

### Takeaway
The camera trick is not a moat. Defensible assets in rising order of strength:
1. the tape-checked error table and site dataset;
2. the versioned IS rulebook with engineer sign-off;
3. **record acceptance**, i.e. lenders, insurers or GBA accepting a Sariya record as evidence (a network effect: more acceptors, more operators, more records);
4. being embedded in a brand's or JSW One's steel flow.

The biggest business risk is the **₹12-19 Cr seat ceiling combined with low SMB willingness to pay** (Powerplay), so the company stalls as a nice tool.

### Cited findings
- Seat ceiling inputs: UltraTech 1,200 civil engineers; Ambuja ~36,000 technical sites in FY26 (gtm.md [V/S]).
- Indian SMB SaaS weakness: Powerplay financials [S above].
- Insurance requires technical inspection [S, Q5]; the NHB staged-disbursal push [S, Q5]: these are the acceptance hooks.

### Inferences: risk register additions [H]

| Risk | Why | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Seat ceiling and brand concentration | 5-8k seats total; 2-3 buyers decide | Price per record; seats include quotas; add the professional channel from day one |
| "WhatsApp photo is free" | The status quo costs ₹0 | Sell the signature and acceptance, not the photo: "a record a lender or insurer accepts" |
| Engineers won't sign evidence against themselves | Liability flip | Owner-pull selling; the record shows "flagged and fixed" as a positive |
| Acceptance never comes | Regulators move slowly | Pilot with one HFC (tranche visits) and one IDI broker in parallel with the brand pilot |
| Clone by a brand or JSW One | Low technical barrier | Be the white-label inside them (BuildNext precedent: they buy, not build, small QA teams) |
| Margin killed by support | 1:2,000 ratio | Brand-run tier-1 support; self-serve rulebook; error table in the app |

### Gaps
- No data on how often owners or lenders would actually request a record. That is the pivotal unknown and the pilot's job.

## Q9. The single business-model slide for this jury (founders, cloud/enterprise architects, iQOO leadership)

### Takeaway
One slide, one unit (the signed pour record), one payer sequence (brand → professional → insurer/lender), one number for size (~8M pours/yr), and one why-now (arrests after collapses, counterfeit TMT, NHB stage-linked disbursal). Founders will probe the ceiling and payer; enterprise architects will probe the record as an API. The slide answers both.

### Draft slide (60 words) [H]
> **One price: per signed pour.** India casts ~8 million RCC slabs a year; each is visible for a day. Steel brands sponsor Sariya records for their customers (₹149); architects and engineers buy them for clients (₹249), resold at ₹1,000+. Every record is signed, error-banded evidence. Next: insurers and lenders paying per verification. Zero marginal compute: it runs on the phone.

**"Who pays, how much, why now" line:**
> Steel brands and the engineers already paid to be at the pour pay ₹149-249 per signed record, because collapses now end in engineers' arrests, fake TMT is being seized, and lenders must tie every tranche to a verified stage.
